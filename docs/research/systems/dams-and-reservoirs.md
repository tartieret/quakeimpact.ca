**Status.** Band **Not yet assessed** in both scenarios — and the reason is the finding.
Dam Safety Reviews of both Metro Vancouver water-supply dams were carried out and
completed in 2024, and their conclusions are published every year in the GVWD Dam Safety
Program Annual Update tabled at the Water Committee. **Both dams were assessed, but not
for this.** Cleveland and Seymour Falls are classified **Extreme** — the province's top
consequence class; both were reviewed by an engineer in 2024 as the law requires; neither
published conclusion mentions earthquakes; and the seismic upgrade project has not
started. The hatched cell does not mean *nobody has looked*. It means *someone looked,
recently and under legal compulsion, and the published answer is silent on earthquakes.*

## Findings

### The consequence classification, and what it means in people

- **Cleveland Dam and Seymour Falls Dam are classified "Extreme" failure consequence** —
  the top of the province's five-tier scale. [BC-DAMS-REG] **[A]** Confirmed by three
  independent routes: the provincial register, a Ministry annual report naming Cleveland
  as Extreme in its 2020 incident entry [BCDSP-AR-2021] **[A]**, and Metro Vancouver's own
  board reporting — *"Cleveland Dam and Seymour Falls Dam are classified as 'extreme'
  consequence dams according to the provincial Dam Safety Regulation."* [MV-DSP-2026]
  **[A]** Unlike the 1-in-10,000-year figure below, this **is** corroboration: three
  separate documents from two levels of government.
- **The class is a consequence rating expressed in people, not a hazard rating.** Extreme
  means potential loss of life of **"more than 100"**, with a population at risk that is
  permanent — defined in the Regulation's own schedule as **"ordinarily or regularly
  located in the dam-breach inundation zone, whether to live, work or recreate."**
  [BCDSR-40-2016] **[A]** That is the owner's own submitted classification, accepted by a
  provincial officer.
- The same classification applies in the region to **Coquitlam, Ruskin, Stave Falls,
  Alouette and Cheakamus**, all BC Hydro's, and to the two **Eagle Lake** dams
  in West Vancouver. [BC-DAMS-REG] **[A]** Cite the register and date the citation — BC
  Hydro's own tables and a 2012 BCUC order disagree with it for Ruskin, and
  the register is the record the Regulation's obligations attach to.
- **Wahleach is Very High, not Extreme.** BC Hydro's own facility asset plan, filed with
  the BCUC, classifies it **Very High**. [BCH-RRA-F2020] **[A]** The distinction is not
  cosmetic: the design criteria the province sets differ by class, and Wahleach's only
  quantified seismic item is its Jones Lake intake gates, at roughly once in 4,800 years.
- **Both dams currently sit at risk level "3 – Stable"** in the register. [BC-DAMS-REG]
  **[A]** This must never travel without its ceiling: **no Extreme dam in BC is rated
  better than 3 – Stable**, so it is the best rating available rather than a distinction
  earned. It is not vacuous either — 20 of BC's 56 Extreme dams are currently rated worse.
  The defensible statement is that the provincial dam safety officer has not currently
  flagged a deficiency at either dam, on a scale where roughly one in three of BC's
  highest-consequence dams currently is flagged. Both halves ship or neither does.

### The Regulation: a review is compulsory, and there is no seismic number in it

- **An Extreme-classification dam requires a Dam Safety Review every seven years**,
  carried out by an engineering professional qualified in dam safety analysis, "to
  determine if the dam is safe", with the report submitted to a dam safety officer for
  acceptance. [BCDSR-40-2016] **[A]** (s.20(1); Schedule 2 item 9. Very high and high:
  every ten years.)
- **Failure to carry out or submit the review is an offence**, punishable by a fine of up
  to **$200,000** or six months' imprisonment, and for a continuing offence up to $200,000
  per day. [BCDSR-40-2016] **[A]** The site can therefore say a review must exist without
  having to find one.
- **The Regulation contains no seismic design standard at all.** Full text searched, 10
  September 2026: "seismic" appears once, in the definition of "instrumentation", and
  refers to monitoring rather than design; "design earthquake", "return period", "ground
  motion", "Canadian Dam Association" and "10,000" return zero hits. [BCDSR-40-2016]
  **[A]** The criterion enters through guidance beneath the Regulation, because s.20
  requires the review to be carried out "in accordance with the requirements of the
  comptroller or a water manager".
- **The Metro Vancouver fact sheets are not a source for what the Regulation contains.**
  They state that the Regulation "requires owners of major dams to ensure their dams
  withstand an extreme earthquake or flood". [MV-CLEVELANDDAM-FS] [MV-SEYMOURDAM-FS]
  **[A]** *for what the fact sheets say* — they are not [A] for what the Regulation
  contains, and the Regulation does not contain it. Treat it as the owner's paraphrase and
  cite the Regulation directly.
- **A Dam Safety Review produces a defined three-valued verdict.** A Qualified Professional
  Engineer signs a Dam Safety Review Assurance Statement attesting that the dam is
  "reasonably safe", "reasonably safe" with deficiencies or non-conformances, or "not
  safe" with deficiencies requiring urgent action; the review must be peer-reviewed by a
  different engineer, and an audit-style review is not accepted without prior written
  approval. [BCDSP-DSR-INFO] **[A]** So what is missing from the public record is not
  vague: it is a specific, dated, sealed document with one of three answers on its face.
  "Reasonably safe" is the formal middle rung, which the province states "is considered by
  the Regulatory Authority to be equivalent to the Dam Safety Regulation term of 'safe'" —
  it is not hedging. [BCDSP-DSR-INFO] **[A]**

### The 1-in-10,000-year standard, with four guards

The province's *Guidelines for Dam Design and Construction*,
Appendix A, Table 3, sets the minimum **Earthquake Design Ground Motion** for an
Extreme-consequence dam at **"1/10,000 or MCE"**. [BC-DDCG] **[A]** That is enough to
print it. It is not enough to call it corroborated, and four guards travel with it.

- **(a) Lineage.** The guideline's own footnote says its table is *"generally adapted from
  CDA Guidelines (CDA, 2007, revised 2013)"*. [BC-DDCG] **[A]** The provincial design
  guideline, the EGBC review guideline [EGBC-DSR-GL], BC Hydro's own dam safety policy as
  quoted in the Ruskin report [BCH-RUSKIN-SEIAM-11] and the media reporting of the figure
  all descend from the same ancestor. **Four documents, one lineage** — and that
  ancestor, [CDA-2007], is a paywalled member publication nobody in this project has read.
  What the multiplicity establishes is that 1/10,000 is the standard actually applied by
  the province and by BC Hydro; what it does not establish is independent confirmation of
  the number.
- **(b) Scope.** The guideline's stated purpose is regulatory requirements "to construct,
  alter, improve, or replace a dam". [BC-DDCG] **[A]** Table 3 is a design criterion for
  new work, alterations and replacements — **not a standing obligation on a 1954 dam.**
  Cleveland was commissioned in 1954 and Seymour Falls in 1961. [BC-DAMS-REG] **[A]**
- **(c) It is a deviable guideline minimum, not law.** Deviations "may be considered but
  must be clearly stated and justified". [BC-DDCG] **[A]** The correct verb is *should*,
  not *must*, and the correct noun is a ministry guideline minimum. The Regulation itself
  carries no seismic number.
- **(d) The criterion reads "1/10,000 **or MCE**".** The Maximum Credible Earthquake is a
  deterministic ceiling, not a probability, and the two are alternatives. A sentence
  reading "must withstand a 1-in-10,000-year earthquake" drops half the criterion and
  promotes a guideline to a statute in the same clause. Safe form: *the province's design
  guideline sets the minimum earthquake design ground motion for an Extreme-consequence dam
  at a 1-in-10,000-year return period or the Maximum Credible Earthquake.*

**What connects that criterion to an old dam** is the Dam Safety Review itself. The EGBC
guideline lists "Maximum Design Earthquake (MDE)" among the "potential updates to the
original design criteria" a review produces, and directs that "potential liquefaction should
also be considered". [EGBC-DSR-GL] **[A]** So a 1954 dam **is** periodically re-measured
against a current design earthquake, every seven years, by a sealed and peer-reviewed
report. The site can say that. It still cannot say what the answer was.

### Cross-page guard: dam return periods are not building return periods

The EGBC guideline states that National Building Code seismic hazard parameters "represent
the median hazard values and are not site-specific", that mean hazard values are
recommended for dam safety engineering, and therefore that NBCC-derived parameters
**"should not be used for dam safety reviews"** — usable only for screening-level analysis
with correction factors and stated limitations. [EGBC-DSR-GL] **[A]**

**Consequence for the site as a whole: a return period on this page is not commensurable
with a return period on the buildings pages.** Dams are assessed on mean, site-specific
hazard; buildings on the NBCC's median grid. A reader who compares 1-in-2,475 on a
buildings page against 1-in-10,000 here is comparing incompatible quantities, and no page
may invite that comparison. This belongs in `../knowledge.md` as a site-wide rule, not only
here.

**The province says the same thing by a different route, so the guard now has two sources.**
The BC dam design and construction guidelines state that NRCan's seismic hazard models "are
appropriate to use for Low, Significant and High consequence dams", may be extrapolated for
screening only at Very High and Extreme, and that "detailed design should use a site specific
Probabilistic Seismic Hazard Assessment (PSHA)". [BC-DDCG] **[A]** EGBC and the province
reach the same rule independently: the national grid is a screening tool for the top two
classes, not a design input.

**And here is why the guard is needed rather than tidy.** The province's own criteria table
sets the **High** consequence class at **1-in-2,475** — numerically identical to the National
Building Code's return period for buildings. [BC-DDCG] **[A]** The two incommensurable scales
*touch* at exactly one row, which makes an accidental comparison more likely, not less. A
reader who notices the coincidence will conclude the scales are the same scale. They are not.

### Ruskin Dam — the region's worked example, deficiency stated in numbers and then fixed

This is the only dam near the region for which a public document states, in numbers, that
the structure could not withstand the required earthquake — and a later public document
states that it can.

- BC Hydro's consultant, **Hemmera, April 2011**: *"Canadian Dam Safety Guidelines and
  current BC Hydro Dam Safety policy both require that the Ruskin Dam must survive the
  Maximum Design Earthquake (MDE) equal to the 1 in 10,000 year exceedance level
  (representing a Peak Ground Acceleration of ~0.7g). Key components of the Dam and
  Powerhouse have seismic withstand levels that are significantly below the MDE levels."*
  The same page records *"up to 300 people downstream of the Dam during summer months"*.
  [BCH-RUSKIN-SEIAM-11] **[A]** for the statement; **[B]** for the underlying "BC Hydro,
  2008" finding it cites, which is unread.
- **The remedy completed.** BC Hydro's F2019 quarterly reporting records that "After a 20
  year Dam Safety initiative, and 11 years of this project, the construction of the upper
  part of the dam (including commissioning of the new gates) was completed in F2018", and
  its Advisory Board concluded the project "Has met the overall project objectives of
  withstanding the Maximum Design Earthquake (1:10,000 Annual Exceedance Frequency)".
  [BCH-RUSKIN-F2019] **[A]** for design intent.

**Guards, all three mandatory.**

- **"Designed to withstand", never "verified to withstand".** The Advisory Board's
  statement is an expert panel's conclusion about the design and the project's objectives.
  There is no independent post-construction verification document. [BCH-RUSKIN-F2019]
- **Ruskin is in Mission, in the Fraser Valley Regional District — not Metro Vancouver.**
  Same owner, same regulator, same standard, and the clause costs one line. [BC-DAMS-REG]
  **[A]**
- **Print no cost figure.** Six variants circulate in BC Hydro's own documents ($856.9M,
  $748M budget, $728.6M, $718.1M, $642M, $636.3M estimate at completion). A figure with six
  public variants is a figure that will be contradicted, and the page needs none of them.
- The 2011 statement describes **a problem since fixed**, and any use must say so in the
  same sentence.

### What BC Hydro says its own dams withstand — the numbers, from its own filings

Retrieved 10 September 2026 from BC Hydro's Facility Asset Plans, filed with the BCUC as
Appendix K to its F2020–F2021 Revenue Requirements Application. [BCH-RRA-F2020] **[A]**
throughout. This is the utility describing its own assets to its own regulator.

**The yardstick, stated by the owner.** *"Given that these are all Extreme consequence dams,
current expectations — as outlined in the Canadian Dam Association's Dam Safety Guidelines —
are that they should be able to withstand an earthquake of intensity expected to occur once
every 10,000 years."* Every figure below should be read against that sentence.

- **Alouette.** Spillway damage "that would render it unsafe for spills or drawdowns after
  the earthquake" at **once every 1,000 to 2,500 years**; right abutment foundation failure
  at **2,500 years**, leading to eventual failure of the concrete weir structures regulating
  flow over the spillway; power tunnel headworks and surge tower failure **expected at once
  every 100 to 200 years**, which "could block the post-earthquake discharge of water from
  Alouette Reservoir to Stave Lake Reservoir"; and a low level outlet conduit running under
  the dam that is seismically deficient, "having unquantifiable withstand".
- **Cheakamus.** *"Insufficient resistance to seismic loads that may lead to failure of the
  dam, spillway, spillway gates and/or penstock pedestals in a major earthquake occurring, on
  average, about once every 1,000 years or more."*
- **Coquitlam.** Tunnel inlet and outlet portals, potential failure at **once every 100 to
  200 years**; the Low Level Outlet structure at **3,500 to 5,000 years**.
- **Wahleach** (Very High, not Extreme). Jones Lake intake gates, potential failure at once
  every **4,800 years**, which "would prevent the closure of the water passage… and could
  result in the inundation of adjacent utility and transportation corridors".

**Two guards, and neither is optional.**

**These are component withstands, not dam-failure probabilities.** Every figure names a
part — a spillway, a tunnel portal, a surge tower — and the shaking at which that part is
expected to fail. Alouette's 1-in-100-to-200-year figure is its power tunnel headworks, not
its dam. Read as *the dam fails at 1-in-150 years* it is false and frightening; read as *the
means of controlling the reservoir after an earthquake fails at 1-in-150 years* it is true,
and in context worse. A sentence that cannot carry the component name does not carry the
number.

**A retained risk is a decision, not an oversight.** BC Hydro names each deficiency, prices
the alternative, and states in writing which ones it is accepting. At Alouette it has chosen
to upgrade the power tunnel rather than the spillway, holding the spillway and abutment
deficiencies as retained risks, with cost, in-service date and construction start all "to be
determined"; at Coquitlam only the inlet portal upgrade is in the plan, and the outlet portal
and Low Level Outlet "are monitored and will be retained as the consequences are expected to
be low". Cheakamus's remedies sit behind its generating-equipment work.

**The single line that carries the whole finding.** Until the tunnel work is done, BC Hydro
states that *"Alouette Lake Reservoir will be operated in a manner that provides sufficient
time to provide emergency response following a major earthquake."* A reservoir operated to
buy evacuation time.

**One dam where the answer is nothing.** Stave Falls and Blind Slough are Extreme, and the
Stave Falls New Facility Asset Plan records **no seismic issue at all** among its significant
remaining risks — its problems are turbine design and control-system obsolescence. Where a
dam has no seismic deficiency, say so; the page is not an argument.

### The regulator's own oversight — September 2021, and it must be dated every time

- The Office of the Auditor General of British Columbia concluded in **September 2021**:
  **"The ministry has not effectively overseen the safety of dams in B.C. While it promoted
  dam owner compliance with regulatory requirements, it did not adequately verify and
  enforce compliance."** [OAG-DAMS-21] **[A]** Nine recommendations, all accepted.
- On reviews specifically: *"The average time between reports being submitted and the
  ministry accepting them was 20 months; some had taken 8 years."* Of three accepted DSRs
  assessed by the OAG's subject-matter expert, two were missing elements required by the
  legislated guidelines. [OAG-DAMS-21] **[A]**
- On audits: *"Overall, 28% of dams in the database were past their next audit date; they
  were overdue by 3.5 years, on average."* And on the register: at least 196 dams missing
  from records; 63% of sampled records lacking key information. [OAG-DAMS-21] **[A]**
- **What this does to the page, both ways.** It strengthens the claim that reviews exist and
  are submitted in volume. It **weakens** any inference from "legally required" to
  "therefore done properly and on time" — the site must never write *the dams are reviewed
  every seven years* as though that settles anything.
- **Implementation, as at 31 March 2025: two of the nine recommendations complete.**
  [OAG-DAMS-FU-25] **[A]** The three editions report 1 of 9 at 30 November 2022, 1 of 9 at
  30 November 2023, and 2 of 9 at 31 March 2025. Dam safety is the worst-performing of the
  2021 audits, against 55% completion across that year's cohort.
- **Read the source table with care.** Its columns run *Total | Not complete | Complete |
  Change*, so the 2025 row reads naturally as 78% complete and means 78% **incomplete**.
  Verified against the per-recommendation detail: only Recommendations 1 and 6 are marked
  completed. Anyone re-deriving this figure must check the same way.
- **What the ministry says, in its own words.** The progress reports are written by the
  Ministry of Water, Land and Resource Stewardship and reviewed by the OAG under s.11(8) of
  the *Auditor General Act* — a limited-assurance review, not an audit, and it must be
  described as one. On staffing: *"An assessment of the staffing needs to administer the DSP
  was completed by the ASDSO … and concluded that 25 staff would be required. The DSP agrees
  with these numbers. DSP staffing levels have deteriorated in 2024 with currently 10 staff
  delivering the program."* On audit frequency: *"due to increasing staff shortages, we are
  not meeting our program objectives under our existing Dam Safety Audit policy (2013).
  Currently we are risk managing which audits we can complete and are only able to target the
  very highest risk dams."* Both carry an anticipated completion of **2028**.
  [OAG-DAMS-FU-25] **[A]**
- **Date guard, mandatory.** The audit period is January 2019 to December
  2020 and the report is September 2021. Cite the *findings* as 2021, and cite
  *implementation* as at **31 March 2025**. A 2021 finding must never be presented as a
  description of 2026, and the follow-up record must not be presented as though the
  recommendations were met.

### Provincial audits of both dams are six years overdue

- Formal audits by the provincial Dam Safety Officer run on an approximately five-year cycle
  for Extreme and Very High dams. **The most recent audits of Cleveland and Seymour Falls
  were completed in 2020**; as of June 2026 follow-up audits "are being planned by the Dam
  Safety Officer for 2026". [MV-DSP-2026] **[A]**
- Six years since the last provincial audit of the region's two highest-consequence
  drinking-water dams, against a five-year cycle. This is the local instance of the OAG's
  province-wide 28%-overdue finding, reached from an independent direction — a genuine
  convergence, not a lineage artefact.
- **The cause is on the record, in the regulator's own words.**
  The province's dam safety programme runs **10 staff against an assessed need of 25**,
  staffing "deteriorated" in 2024, and audits are explicitly triaged — "only able to target
  the very highest risk dams". [OAG-DAMS-FU-25] **[A]** The overdue audits are not an
  oversight; they are what a programme at 40% of its assessed staffing does on purpose.
- **The public annual report is deprioritised for the same reason**, which is why no Dam

### The October 2020 incident — non-seismic, and included for what it does bear on

**This section is not about earthquakes. Nothing in any source connects it to seismic
performance, and nothing on the site may use it to imply anything about how either dam
would behave in one.** It is here because it bears directly on two things the site does
cover: whether the public downstream of an Extreme dam is warned, and how the oversight
regime performs.

- On **1 October 2020**, during maintenance work on the sensors controlling it, the radial
  drum gate of Cleveland Dam opened unexpectedly. The resulting uncontrolled release into
  the Capilano River **killed two people** — reported as a father and son — and **three
  other anglers were rescued**. [BCDSP-AR-2021] **[A]** [OAG-DAMS-21] **[A]** (These two
  are both provincial documents from the same period and are one account with two
  expressions, not two sources.)
- **The cause, in the owner's words:** *"the clearest contributing factor was human error
  related to programming of the control system for the spillway gate"*, during drum gate
  maintenance. The staff responsible had their employment terminated.
  [MV-CLEVELAND-UPDATES] **[A]** [BCDSP-AR-2021] **[A]** The structure itself was
  undamaged. [MV-CLEVELAND-UPDATES] **[A]**
- **No charges, no orders, no published report.** Metro Vancouver records that the RCMP and
  the Crown prosecutor confirmed no charges would be filed, and that WorkSafeBC accepted its
  incident investigation report "with no further requirements or orders".
  [MV-CLEVELAND-UPDATES] **[B]** — this is the utility's account of others' decisions; no
  BC Prosecution Service statement was located. The provincial compliance and enforcement
  database returns **zero records** for "Cleveland" across all eleven public datasets.
  [NRCED] **[A]** on the absence. **Metro Vancouver's investigation report was never
  published**, and no board report reviewing the incident exists in any retrieved agenda or
  minute. [MV-DSP-2026] **[A]** on the absence. **Do not imply a regulatory outcome — there
  was not one.**
- **The 2002 warning.** A **WorkSafeBC inspection report of 18 July 2002**, released under
  freedom of information and reported by CBC and Global News, recorded *"recent and past
  history of inadvertent and uncontrolled operations of the spillway gate causing dangerous
  sudden rises and increased flows in the river"* and ordered measures including spillway
  gate lockout and **public warning signage and warning alarms**. [CBC-WSBC-2002] **[B]**
- **The alarms were never installed.** Reported in the same coverage: *"Such a warning
  system was never installed because of concerns the noise would annoy residents in the
  Glenmore and Grouse Woods neighbourhoods less than 400 metres away, according to
  engineering reports from the early 2000s."* [CBC-WSBC-2002] **[B]** Eighteen years later
  the two deaths above occurred.
- **The 2002 material stays [B] and its route is named in every sentence that uses it.** We
  have not read the underlying inspection report or the early-2000s engineering reports;
  they are leads, not sources. The attribution must read "a 2002 WorkSafeBC inspection
  report, released under freedom of information and reported by CBC", every time.
- **What existed by way of warning in 2020**, in the owner's own words on 5 October 2020:
  *"The two dams that we operate, Seymour Falls Dam and Cleveland Dam, do not have external,
  public-facing alarm systems."* [MV-CLEVELAND-UPDATES] **[A]** One is now being built — the
  **Capilano River Evacuation System**, target completion 2027, with lights, sirens and
  river level gauges, alarms tested silently daily and audibly monthly. [MV-CDSEP]
  [MV-DSP-2026] **[A]** **Guard: those alarms are for unscheduled dam releases. They do not
  sound for freshet or heavy rainfall, and they are not a dam-breach or earthquake warning
  system.** [MV-CDSEP] **[A]**
- **Two documents remain unretrieved**, and both would be the most detailed public accounts
  if they exist: a **BC Coroners Service report or inquest** into the two deaths, not located
  in any channel; and the underlying **2002 WorkSafeBC inspection report**. **[?]**
- **How far this may be pushed: not far.** What it establishes is that an Extreme-consequence
  dam released a lethal surge into an inhabited canyon through normal appurtenances operating
  wrongly, with the structure entirely intact — and that the canyon below Cleveland Dam is
  occupied, which is the concrete meaning of "population ordinarily or regularly located in
  the dam-breach inundation zone". To write "an earthquake could do this" is inference, not
  evidence, and the style guide forbids it.

### Inundation mapping — neither withheld nor absent

- **The mapping exists.** An owner of a significant-or-higher dam must prepare a dam
  emergency plan and submit it for acceptance; the province's guidance states that Extreme
  dams' engineering consultants "may have prepared a detailed inundation or evacuation area
  map", and the DEP template requires an evacuation area map to be inserted.
  [BCDSR-40-2016] [BCDSP-DEP-24] **[A]** by requirement, not by sighting. Both dams' DEPs
  were updated in March 2026. [MV-DSP-2026] **[A]**
- **Local emergency authorities receive a defined part, not the whole.** s.9(6) delivers only
  the record prepared for local emergency authorities' use; the province's controlled-copy
  register gives the dam owner, the emergency dam contact and the dam safety officer the
  *whole* plan and the local emergency contact a *part* — "Sections 1, 2 & 4.1 and Appendix
  A (A-1, A-2 & A-3) only". [BCDSR-40-2016] [BCDSP-DEP-24] **[A]** Metro Vancouver's term
  for the recipients is "external DEP plan holders". [MV-DSP-2025] **[A]**
- **There is no publication duty and no confidentiality clause.** Searched with no
  confidentiality, security or non-disclosure provision of any kind found bearing on dam
  emergency plans or inundation studies: the full consolidated B.C. Reg. 40/2016; the
  province's *Estimating Dam Break Downstream Inundation*; the Consequence of Failure
  Classification Guideline; and BC Hydro's dam-safety pages. [BCDSR-40-2016]
  [BCDSP-INUND-16] **[A]** The regime routes these documents to the regulator and, in part,
  to local emergency authorities, and **never contemplates public release**. That is a third
  answer, and it is neither of the two the conventions anticipate. It closes the question
  rather than sharpening it.
- The nearest thing to a policy statement is the province's FIPPA s.25 position: proactive
  disclosure attaches to **Risk Level 1 ("Alert") dams**, and neither of these is one.
  [BCDSP-AR-2324] **[A]** That is suggestive of discretion rather than prohibition; it is
  **not** a statement about inundation maps and must not be presented as one.
- **The visible asymmetry, and it is the best available line.** BC Hydro publishes
  **dam-failure evacuation brochures for Campbell River and the Strathcona Regional
  District** (both 2014, both Vancouver Island), and a community flood map was prepared for
  the Squamish Valley / Daisy Lake. **Nothing comparable exists for Ruskin, Stave Falls,
  Alouette, Coquitlam or Wahleach** — four of them Extreme-consequence dams in the Lower
  Mainland, or for either Metro Vancouver dam. [BCH-DAMFAQ] **[B]** on the asymmetry as
  observed across published channels. The same utility publishes dam-failure evacuation
  material for communities on Vancouver Island and not for communities below its Lower
  Mainland dams.
- **Named Cleveland downstream-hazard studies exist and are unpublished.** Metro Vancouver's
  2021 update lists, for the 2022–2024 programme, "a river users study, river hydraulic
  modelling, downstream hazards studies, updated public safety risk assessment, and the
  design and construction of a long term public warning system". [MV-DSP-2022] **[A]** These
  are named, dated, commissioned studies of exactly the question a reader asks, and none is
  published. **This is the most specific FOI target this pass produced.**
- **Do not claim municipal-level absence.** The municipal leg of this search was blocked —
  `mission.ca`, `coquitlam.ca` and `metrovancouver.org` search all returned JavaScript
  shells with no indexable text, `fvrd.ca` returned 403, and a Wayback enumeration timed
  out. A municipality may publish an evacuation map this pass could not see.
- **Earthquake is an explicit trigger in dam emergency planning.** The DEP template's
  escalation table lists "Measurable earthquake felt or reported on or within 50 kilometers
  of the dam", "Earthquake resulting in visible damage to the dam or appurtenances" and
  "Earthquake resulting in uncontrolled release of water from the dam". [BCDSP-DEP-24]
  **[A]**

## For the page

**Mechanism paragraph for the impact cell (both scenarios, band Not yet assessed).**

> Cleveland and Seymour Falls dams are classified Extreme — the province's top consequence
> class, meaning more than a hundred people could die and that people ordinarily live, work
> or recreate below both. Both were reviewed by an engineer in 2024, as the law requires
> every seven years, and Metro Vancouver reports that both were found safe. Neither
> published conclusion mentions earthquakes. The $25 million project to bring Cleveland Dam
> up to the Maximum Credible Earthquake standard has not started, and is scheduled to run
> from 2028 to 2034.

[BC-DAMS-REG] [BCDSR-40-2016] [MV-DSP-2026] [MV-CAPEX-2026] — all **[A]**.

That paragraph records that work was done, says when, says what it found, and says exactly
what it did not cover — without a number the sources do not carry and without a hedge. It
is the spine of the page. **The hatched cell means "assessed, but not for this", and the
copy must make that distinction do the work.** It is not "we don't know".

**Balance is a requirement, not a courtesy.** A page carrying the 2028–2034 date and the
overdue provincial audits without also carrying the surveillance regime — weekly
surveillance, semi-annual formal inspections, instrumentation reviewed daily, every interval
matching Schedule 2 — is slanting the evidence. The Howell-Bunger valves are the same shape:
seventy-year-old original equipment, advanced deterioration found in 2016, **replaced in
2021**. Both halves ship or neither does.

**The worked example: lead with Ruskin, keep Coquitlam for the mechanism.** Ruskin has a
primary source, a named standard, a ground-motion figure, a downstream population and a
documented completion — deficiency stated in 2011 reporting a 2008 finding, remedy complete
F2018, twenty years from initiative to finish. Coquitlam supplies the liquefaction mechanism
and the cross-reference to the ground-conditions page; Ruskin supplies the arc. Cross-refer
rather than re-explain.

**Guards that travel with the numbers.**

- **1-in-10,000 years:** all four guards, every time — one lineage (CDA 2007) not four
  sources; a criterion for new work, not a standing duty on a 1954 dam; a deviable guideline
  minimum, not law; and it reads "1/10,000 **or** MCE".
- **Cross-page:** dam return periods are not commensurable with building return periods.
  Never place 1-in-2,475 and 1-in-10,000 where a reader will compare them.
- **Ruskin:** "designed to withstand", never "verified"; in Mission, not Metro Vancouver;
  **no cost figure**.
- **Coquitlam:** **no 30 m × 300 m dimensions.**
- **Capital figures:** **never $80.5M** — it is Burnaby Mountain Tank No. 2 and No. 3's
  money. Trace a figure to its row; do not average two passes that disagree.
- **The Auditor General:** date it in the sentence. September 2021, audit period 2019–2020,
  all nine recommendations accepted, two of nine implemented as at 31 March 2025.
- **"3 – Stable":** never without its ceiling caveat.
- **"Caution" ratings:** the deficiency behind one is not public and is never described as
  seismic.
- **The Capilano alarm:** for unscheduled releases, not dam breach, not earthquakes.
- **The safety boom contract:** debris barriers, never a seismic reassessment.
- **The 2020 incident and the 2002 warning:** label non-seismic wherever they appear. They
  bear on warning and on oversight, and on nothing else. Write them factually. Two people
  died and the site is not going to be coy about it, but it is also not going to use their
  deaths for effect — no adjectives doing work the facts already do, no rhetorical question,
  no implication about earthquakes. The style guide's rule against fear appeals is tested
  exactly here.

**The lever.** The reader's question —
*am I below one of these?* — has a real answer with a real route. The inundation zone is
mapped; it had to be, because the classification depends on it and the dam emergency plan
contains it, and both plans were updated in March 2026. Downstream municipalities are
"external DEP plan holders", though by the province's own controlled-copy register they
receive a defined part of the plan rather than the whole. The public receives none of it,
and nobody has said why: there is no confidentiality clause anywhere in the regime and no
publication duty either. A reader who wants to know can ask their municipality or file an
FOI with Metro Vancouver — and there is no disclosure log, so someone has to be first.
**Send the reader to ask rather than assert that asking is necessary**: the municipal leg of
our search was blocked, and a municipality may already publish something.

**The publication asymmetry is the line to use on mapping**, not any assertion about
withholding: the same utility publishes dam-failure evacuation brochures for Campbell River
and the Strathcona Regional District and none for any of its Lower Mainland Extreme dams.
