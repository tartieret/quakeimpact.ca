The register. Every source key used anywhere in `docs/research/` resolves here, and
every key here is used somewhere. A key in a file and absent from this table is a
defect; a row here that nothing cites is dead weight and is removed.

This file becomes the `REFERENCES` export in `src/content/references.ts`. That file is
generated by `scripts/build-references.mjs`, run as `npm run references`, and is never
edited by hand, so a corrected title, date, URL, route or licence here propagates to the
citation popovers and to `/sources/` in one edit. The generator throws rather than
guessing, so a row with the wrong number of columns, a duplicate key, an unknown route
or an empty Source cell stops the build.

**A row is not a page entry.** Every row generates a reference, and `/sources/` lists the
ones the site cites: a document behind a finding whose page is not written yet, or a search
that returned nothing, belongs here and does not belong in front of a reader who has no
claim to check it against. `src/content/cited.ts` works that out from what the pages and
the band grid actually cite, so a row arrives on `/sources/` in the same edit that first
cites it and nothing has to be marked here.

Two consequences for how a row is written. **Every cell is copy.** The Source cell's
editorial note and the URL cell's prose are rendered to the reader, so they follow
`../style-guide.md` and not research shorthand. And **the register's defects are the
site's defects**: a row with no usable URL renders as a source with no link to follow,
which is honest only if the row says why.

Keys follow `CONVENTIONS.md`: `SCOPE-TOPIC-YY`.

### Notes that are not for the reader

A note written to a colleague reaches a neighbour: it is rendered in the citation
popover on every page that cites the key, and again on `/sources/`. So a note says what
the document is being used for and what it does and does not support, in the reader's
terms. A confidence marker, a superseded key, a route back through an unindexed
document host, how a PDF was extracted, what to obtain next: none of that means
anything to a reader, and none of it is deleted either.

It goes in an HTML comment in the same cell, which the generator strips:

```
| `KEY` | *Title*: what the document supports <!-- research: [A] on section
totals only; per-project figures do not survive extraction. Use OTHER-KEY --> | … |
```

The record stays one line from the row it belongs to, and stays greppable. A comment
may not contain a pipe. Both rendered cells, Source and URL, accept one.

## How to read a row

### Route

**Route** says how we reached the claim, which is what makes a confidence marker
auditable. A marker certifies a route; without the route recorded, `[A]` and `[B]` are
assertions about our own diligence rather than facts about the record.

| Route | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `direct` | The primary document itself, read by us. |
| `media` | Journalism, trade press or commentary reporting an underlying document we have not read. |
| `vendor` | A supplier, contractor, consultancy or proponent describing its own work. |
| `landing` | A project, portal or programme page — a page *about* a thing rather than a document stating the claim. |
| `archive` | A data repository or archival record. |

Two rules follow. A `media`, `vendor` or `landing` row cannot carry `[A]` for an
engineering fact — only for the existence of the statement. And a row whose URL is
marked **paywalled** or **403 to automated fetch** cannot be re-verified by anyone
following the link, which is a property of the claim and not a footnote about our tools.

### Licence

`—` means ordinary citation: the facts are free to state, the expression is not, and the
site paraphrases and links. Anything else is a per-dataset position recorded in
`../licensing.md`, which governs. **Nothing is reproduced, hosted or derived from unless
its licence is named here.**

- **OGL–Canada** — Open Government Licence – Canada. Attribution string and the required
  Open File citation in `../licensing.md`.
- **OGL–Vancouver** / **OGL–BC** — the City and Province equivalents. OGL–BC applies only
  where a record states it; it is not a blanket licence for `gov.bc.ca`.
- **ICLR custom terms** — MVSMMP. Non-commercial, attribution, share-alike on GIS layers,
  map sheets unalterable, ICLR's prior written approval reserved for commercial and
  electronic publication. **Blocked pending that approval.**
- **All rights reserved (Province of BC)** — the Province's default, recorded at
  `BC-COPYRIGHT`. It is not OGL–BC, and a `gov.bc.ca` domain confers no licence. Facts
  may be stated and short passages quoted with attribution; nothing is reproduced. The DCRRA goes further — its host declares
  **"Access Only"** (`DCRRA-LICENCE`). Legislation on BC Laws is the exception, under the
  **King's Printer Licence – British Columbia**, and may be quoted at length.

### Dates

Dates are the document's own publication date where verified. `undated` means the page
carries no date; `not recovered` means verification was attempted in the September 2026
pass and failed.

## Why the DCRRA is three rows

`DCRRA-2025`, `DCRRA-EXP` and `DCRRA-APPC` are **three rows for one publication**, and
they stay three rows.

They are kept apart because the research files cite them for different things, at
different page ranges, with different authorship and different confidence consequences.
`DCRRA-2025` is the Provincial Report's own narrative and ch. 5 scenario; `DCRRA-EXP` is
the ch. 2 exposure analysis and its 2,475-year hazard-zone method, which must never be
read as a scenario result; `DCRRA-APPC` is Appendix C, whose case studies are signed by
named UBC, VCH and consulting authors and are the closest thing the province publishes to
peer-reviewed work. Collapsing the three would let a ch. 2 exposure percentage and a ch. 5
scenario casualty count appear under one citation, which is precisely the merge the
conventions forbid.

The cost is that a reader following three keys reaches one document. That is cheaper than
the alternative. Each row above names its chapter and its file, so the three resolve to
one publication without pretending to be one claim.

A fourth DCRRA document exists and is **not** registered here: the *Hazard Threat Analysis
Main Report*, keyed `DCRRA` in an earlier draft register, at
`https://nrs.objectstore.gov.bc.ca/xedyjn/Projects/2024/DCRRA/reports/final/DCRRA%20Hazard%20Threat%20Analysis%20Main%20Report%20(1).pdf`.
It is a different document from the Provincial Report and nothing in `docs/research/`
cites it. It is recorded here so that the bare key `DCRRA` is never reused for the
Provincial Report by mistake.

## What cannot be re-verified by following a link

Nine rows reach the reader with no link at all, and the row itself says why. Each is a
claim a reader cannot re-verify by following a link, which is a fact about the evidence
and belongs in `open-questions.md` rather than being smoothed over here.

Four were read and their paths were not captured: `BCH-COQUITLAM-RRA-06`,
`BCH-RRA-F2020` and `BCH-RRA-F2023` on `docs.bcuc.com`, which has no index and whose
paths cannot be guessed, and `QB-MOTH-95` from an Internet Archive copy of a `gov.bc.ca`
contracting PDF. `WYLLIE-NORRISH` is a consultant report with no publisher, no DOI and no
public copy, known only from its citation in the environmental assessment. `MV-CH2M-18` is
not lost but deliberately withheld, which is a different fact again. And `CHCH-POP` names
a subject rather than a document: no publication carrying the figure has been identified,
so it has no route and nothing may be cited to it.

The last two used to carry a link that was not the document. `UBCM-DCRRA` was reached only
as far as `www.ubcm.ca`, and `BCH-RUSKIN-F2019` was read through a `bchydro.com` document
library whose project page now 404s. Neither host is written as a URL any more, because a
row that renders as having a link should have one that reaches the document.

`AIR-2013` and `RMS-KOBE` were recovered from Internet Archive captures and carry the
`archive` route — their publishers' own copies are gone, which is itself a fact about the
record. `CBOC-2016` is recorded as **not retrievable**, its figures reaching us only
