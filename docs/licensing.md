# Licensing

Checked 10 September 2026. Supersedes open question 1 and 2 in the research report.*

---

## Rule

**Nothing goes on the site unless its licence has been checked and recorded in the register below.** Where a licence is unconfirmed, the default is to link out, not to reproduce or host.

This is not caution for its own sake. The site's whole proposition is that an emergency planner or engineer reading it finds nothing to object to. A licence breach is the one failure that would get it taken down rather than corrected.

## Cleared for use now

### Open Government Licence – Canada

**Covers:** the NRCan / Geological Survey of Canada National Earthquake Scenario Catalogue, including the M9.0 Cascadia Full Rupture and M7.0 Georgia Strait ShakeMaps, damage and casualty indicators, and the RiskProfiler interface. Also the Probabilistic Seismic Risk Model for Canada (CanadaSRM1).

**Permits:** copying, modifying, publishing, adapting, distributing, in any medium, for any lawful purpose, including commercially. Worldwide, royalty-free, perpetual, non-exclusive.

**Requires:** attribution, and a link to the licence. The catalogue additionally requires citation of the underlying Open File.

**Attribution string to use:**

> Contains information licensed under the Open Government Licence – Canada.
> Hobbs, T.E., Journeay, J.M., Rotheram, D., 2021. An Earthquake Scenario Catalogue for Canada: A Guide to Using Scenario Hazard and Risk Results; Geological Survey of Canada, Open File 8806, 22 p.

**Also state, wherever catalogue output appears:** the modelling covers damage to buildings and their inhabitants from shaking only. It excludes critical infrastructure and vehicles, and excludes losses from aftershocks, liquefaction, landslides and fire following. This is a condition of using it honestly, not of the licence.

**Verify per dataset before download.** OGL–Canada is stated at the catalogue level. Confirm it on each release asset rather than assuming it propagates.

### Open Government Licence – Vancouver

**Covers:** City of Vancouver Open Data Portal and VanMap datasets, including the Dedicated Fire Protection System water mains layer.

Based on version 2.0 of the Open Government Licence – British Columbia, with references to the Province replaced by the City.

**Permits:** copying, modifying, publishing, translating, adapting, distributing or otherwise using the information in any medium, mode or format for any lawful purpose. Worldwide, royalty-free, perpetual, non-exclusive, **including for commercial purposes**.

**Requires:** acknowledgement of the source using any attribution statement the provider specifies, and where possible a link to the licence.

**Attribution string to use:**

> Contains information licensed under the Open Government Licence – Vancouver.

**Does not permit:** any use suggesting official status or that the City endorses the site. Keep the DFPS coverage page factual and clearly independent.

## Not cleared — link only

### MVSMMP (Metro Vancouver Seismic Microzonation Mapping Project)

**Status: link only. Decided 10 September 2026 — the site does not pursue ICLR approval.**

The maps and data layers are published on Borealis under custom dataset terms, not an open licence. In summary:

- Reproduction is permitted for personal, non-commercial, academic and research purposes, with prescribed credit.
- Map sheets may be shared and reproduced only as unaltered PDF.
- The GIS data layers may be shared and adapted, but on a share-alike basis: any remix, transformation or derived material must be made available under the same terms.
- Authorisation for commercial use or commercial publication of the maps, data, or statements, conclusions and abstracts regarding them, through any print or electronic media, is reserved by the Institute for Catastrophic Loss Reduction and requires ICLR's prior written approval.
- A prescribed citation string is required, per map.
- A disclaimer must accompany any use: the maps are for regional purposes only and must not be used for site-specific evaluation.
**Why we do not use them.** The site is free, carries no advertising and generates no revenue, which reads as non-commercial in ordinary usage. But the reservation is ICLR's phrasing, it explicitly covers electronic media, and it extends to statements and conclusions regarding the maps — not just the maps themselves. That is broad enough that the judgement would not be ours to make, and the decision is to not make it: **link to the maps, describe the findings in our own words, and build the site's own graphics from open data.**

**Two constraints that would have survived approval anyway**, which is part of why the approval was not worth chasing:

1. Map sheets are unalterable. The intended design — ground conditions as the base layer, in the site's own palette, municipal outlines as reference only — cannot be built from the published map sheets. It has to be built from the data layers.
2. Building from the data layers makes our derived layer share-alike. Any liquefaction layer we generate would have to be published under MVSMMP's terms, with the citation and disclaimer attached.
**What the site does instead:** link to `https://metrovanmicromap.ca/` and to the individual Borealis DOIs. Describe findings in our own words with normal citation. **Do not host, reproduce, restyle or derive.** Ground conditions becomes a text page with an outbound link, and the flagship graphic is the Dedicated Fire Protection System coverage boundary, which is OGL–Vancouver and fully usable.

**If this is ever reopened**, the route is a written enquiry to Sheri Molnar at Western and to ICLR, describing the site in two sentences and requesting written confirmation; the Borealis download guestbook asks what the map will be used for, which is the natural opening. Recorded so the work is not redone from scratch, not as a pending action.

**Note:** the provincial seismic microzonation portal republishes MVSMMP material. That does not change ICLR's terms. Republication by a government hub does not launder the licence.

### Everything else on a `gov.bc.ca` domain — including the DCRRA and PEIRS

**Status: cite and link. Do not reproduce.** Checked 10 September 2026, and the
answer corrects a working assumption that ran through the whole research pass.

**The Province's default is all rights reserved.** Its copyright page states:
"Copyright © 2026, Province of British Columbia. All rights reserved. This
material is owned by the Government of British Columbia and protected by
copyright law. It may not be reproduced or redistributed without the prior
written permission of the Province of British Columbia." Exactly three exceptions
are named: the King's Printer Licence for legislation on BC Laws, a news-footage
licence for specified BC Gov News video, and BC Data Catalogue materials, which
carry either a licence agreement or "access only".

**OGL–BC is not a default. It is applied to specific datasets.** Assuming
otherwise is the mistake to avoid.

**The DCRRA is explicitly closed.** Its host site's configuration reads: "Licensed
under Access Only. These data are Copyright © Province of British Columbia. All
rights reserved." The report PDF itself carries no copyright notice, no licence
statement, no ISBN and no suggested citation — verified against the full text, not
a sample. A BC Data Catalogue search returns no record for it, so no exception
applies.

**What this permits, which is most of what the site needs:**

- **Facts are not copyrightable.** The site may state that the DCRRA gives 3,400
  fatalities, that PEIRS gives 70,000 displaced households, that the All-Hazard
  Plan says 72 hours — and build its own presentation of them.
- **Short quotation with attribution and a link** remains ordinary fair dealing.
  The research folder's practice is unaffected.

**What this forbids:**

- Reproducing any figure, table, map or chart as laid out. Not screenshotted, not
  redrawn, not "adapted from".
- Treating a provincial PDF as reusable because it is public.

**"All rights reserved" does not override fair dealing, and this file said so too
faintly.** Corrected 10 September 2026. The Province's default matters for *reproduction*
— datasets, figures, tables, maps, whole documents. It does not turn quotation into
infringement. Canadian fair dealing covers research, private study, education, criticism
and review, and quoting a published government document with attribution and a link is the
ordinary case, not the edge case.

**The test is the *CCH* factors, and provincial emergency-management text passes them
comfortably**: the purpose is public education; the amount is a passage from a document of
well over a hundred pages; the work is a published planning document intended to be
disseminated; and the effect on the market is nil, because the document is free and a
quotation substitutes for nothing.

**The worked example — the PEIRS experience narrative.** Three paragraphs describing the
first minutes of the crustal M7.0, quoted in full in `research/scenarios.md` under *The
crustal M7.0*. **Publish it under fair dealing, with attribution, a link and the page
number.**

**Where the line sits is judgment, and the file should say so rather than imply a rule.**
Four sentences is unambiguously fine. Three paragraphs is a longer extract of a
qualitatively significant passage — defensible, and not a certainty. Nobody here is a
lawyer. If the project ever wants certainty rather than a good argument, that is a
question for one.

**Ask anyway, in parallel, and not as a gate.** A short note to EMCR costs one email and
the likely answer is yes — this is the province's own preparedness messaging, and a site
amplifying it with credit is the outcome the document exists for. It also opens a channel
to the people who hold several documents this project has recorded as unpublished. Do not
wait on the reply to publish.

Permission route: the Copyright Permission Request Form, `QPIPPCopyright@gov.bc.ca`.
Allow lead time.

**Legislation is the exception that works in our favour.** Statutory text on BC
Laws is under the King's Printer Licence – British Columbia, so the Emergency and
Disaster Management Act may be quoted at length with attribution.

### AIR Worldwide 2013 study — published openly, licensed restrictively

**Status: quote briefly with attribution. Do not reproduce.**

The AIR Worldwide study commissioned by the Insurance Bureau of Canada carries an
explicit notice: "No part of this document may be reproduced or transmitted in any
form, for any purpose, without the express written permission of AIR Worldwide",
and describes itself as containing "proprietary and confidential information …
intended for the exclusive use of AIR clients."

It was nonetheless published on IBC's public asset host for roughly a decade. That
host no longer resolves it, and the working copy is an Internet Archive capture.

The position: short attributed quotation for factual reporting is ordinary
practice and the site's findings from it are facts, not expression. But do not
reproduce its tables, figures or maps, and do not host a copy. **Refresh trigger:
if IBC republishes the study, cite IBC rather than the Archive.**

## Text, figures and reports

Separate from data licensing, and it applies everywhere.

Facts and figures are not themselves protected — restoration times, tonnages, failure counts and dates can be stated freely with citation. Their **expression** is protected. So:

- Paraphrase. Never reproduce paragraphs, tables or figures from reports, journal articles or news coverage.
- Do not reconstruct a source's structure, headings or narrative order.
- Quote only where exact wording carries meaning that paraphrase would lose — official positions, statutory language, direct statements of policy. Keep quotes short and attributed.
- Link to the source rather than summarising it at length. A two-sentence characterisation plus a link is both safer and better practice.
This matters most for the sources reached through media reporting — the water main failure count, the DCRRA casualty figures, the fuel days-of-supply figure. Cite the underlying document where we can obtain it, and name the reporting route where we cannot.

## What the site must publish

A `/licences/` page, linked from the footer of every page, containing:

- The attribution strings above, each with a link to its licence text
- A list of every dataset used, with source, date accessed, and licence
- Any disclaimer required by a source
Standard practice, cheap to build, and it signals to the professional audience that the sourcing discipline is real.

## Build order amendment

The licensing position changes the MVP. Ground conditions was to be the opening page, built on the microzonation liquefaction layer. That layer is blocked.

**Revised MVP, using only cleared sources:**

1. **Scenarios** — moves to first. Built entirely on the NRCan catalogue, both scenarios, fully open, no licensing risk. It was always the site's strongest structural idea and it can now carry the opening.
2. **Ground conditions** — narrative and figures from the peer-reviewed literature, cited normally. Map presence via outbound link to metrovanmicromap.ca. Publishable without MVSMMP reproduction, just weaker visually than intended.
3. **Water** — text only. No licensing dependency.
4. **Transportation** — text only. Disaster Response Routes described, with an outbound link.
5. **Power** — text only.
6. **Method and band rubric** — ships in the MVP. Six of twelve systems are in the fourth band; without the rubric the site reads as unfinished rather than honest.
7. **Preparing** — text only.
**Fire following** gains value under this constraint. The DFPS mains layer is OGL–Vancouver and fully usable, which makes the coverage boundary the first real map we can draw ourselves. **It is the flagship graphic**, in place of the liquefaction layer.

**Not built:** the liquefaction base layer, the ground-conditions choropleth, and the critical-infrastructure-over-liquefaction overlay described in the project document. The overlay was the site's best original graphic and the decision of 10 September 2026 gives it up rather than hold the map work open indefinitely. Ground conditions ships as text with an outbound link.
